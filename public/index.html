Why RIMBUX

Verify First. Execute Safely.

Trust, but verify: give AI money without giving it the keys to everything.

THE PROBLEM

AI agents are starting to spend your money

AI agents can now make payments for you. But a malicious prompt, a scam address, or a bad setting can put your funds at risk, and an AI should never approve its own payments.

THE SOLUTION

Fixed rules, not AI judgment

Simple, fixed rules (not the AI) check every payment against your spending limits, who is receiving the money, and known scam patterns. Payments above the approval threshold wait for a human.

HOW IT WORKS
  1. Understand — Turn the agent’s request into a clear payment (who, how much).
  2. Check — Check spending limits, whether the recipient looks trustworthy, and scam warning signs.
  3. Decide — Decide: allow, ask a human, or block.
  4. Test-sign — Simulate the signature to show what would happen.

How RIMBUX Defends, Layer by Layer

Three layers of defense: a scam filter, a limited-access key, and an on-chain vault. This demo exercises the first two; the vault contract is not inspected here.

Layer 1: Scam filter
Stops scams before signing

Blocks hidden prompt attacks, payments split across many recipients, and lookalike addresses (like al1ce vs alice) before anything is approved.

Layer 2: Limited key
The agent’s key can only do one thing

Design: the agent only holds a limited-access key (NEAR FunctionCall) that can call just execute_agent_payment (no deposit, 0.25 NEAR gas allowance). Use Connect Testnet to verify a real key on-chain.

Layer 3: On-chain vault
Limits enforced on-chain

Design goal: a vault contract enforces payment limits and approved recipients on-chain, even if the agent is compromised. This demo does not inspect that contract.
